## Design Specificity Verdict

Pass. The near-black / warm-paper / orange system, three-front cube, Portuguese operational copy, and evidence-led sequencing are specific to HAWKS BI. The detector's Instrument Sans warning is a false positive in this context: the project identity documentation explicitly commits to Instrument Sans, and replacing it would weaken the supplied brand system.

## Cognitive Load

Low-to-moderate: 2 checklist failures. The first viewport has one clear task path, the selector exposes only three choices, and the content is chunked well. The main extraneous load is the tall desktop scroll-controlled hero and the large cube surface on phone widths, where the object can occupy more attention than the proposition.

## Emotional Journey

The page opens with confidence and a memorable mechanism, moves into a credible operational framing, then resolves in a direct orange contact section. The valley is between the hero and proof: the visitor is asked to interpret the cube before seeing a concrete example or verified outcome. The current active-front detail helps, but mobile needs the object to recede without losing the sense of a living system.

## Strengths

- Ownable brand language: the warm paper/orange/black palette and Instrument Sans geometry feel intentional.
- Strong information architecture: proposition → operational outcome → three fronts → method → contact.
- Thoughtful interaction foundation: direct front selection, roving radio focus, Escape-to-close mobile navigation, and WebGL fallback.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The mobile hero gives the cube too much real estate

Why it matters: at 320–560px the stage and cube reserve roughly half of the opening flow, pushing the proposition and contact path down while the 3D payload is least valuable on a touch device. Fix: hide the WebGL cube below a content-driven mobile breakpoint, retain the active front readout and selector as an editorial signal, and use a restrained progress/line motion to keep the hero alive.

Suggested command: `$impeccable adapt` + `$impeccable animate`

### [P1] The desktop scroll tunnel makes the mechanism feel mandatory

Why it matters: the pinned hero and snap points create a strong authored moment, but visitors who want to scan may have to pass through the object before reaching proof. Fix: reduce the pinned distance and make the direct selector the obvious alternative; keep motion as continuity, not a gate.

Suggested command: `$impeccable layout` + `$impeccable animate`

### [P2] Proof is persuasive but still abstract

Why it matters: “O resultado não é uma tela bonita” frames the offer well, but there is no concrete constraint → intervention → outcome example, so a first-time buyer must trust the positioning before acting. Fix: add one verified operational example per front when factual client evidence is available; do not invent metrics.

Suggested command: `$impeccable clarify`

### [P2] The reduced-motion path removes most authored continuity

Why it matters: the page remains usable, but people who prefer reduced motion lose the cue that the cube/readout and selector form one system. Fix: preserve state with a static selected-front treatment and a small non-animated progress indicator; keep the DOM visible by default.

Suggested command: `$impeccable animate`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan, first-time operations lead:** Understands the three service fronts but must infer proof from broad statements; the page does not yet show a concrete before/after signal.
- **Alex, keyboard visitor:** Selector and menu focus behavior are solid, but there is no skip link and the large pinned hero adds extra tab/scroll context before the core content.
- **Maya, mobile decision-maker:** The phone layout stacks correctly, yet the cube stage and selector become a long preamble before services; hiding the cube while keeping the selector/readout would preserve character and improve scanability.

## Minor Observations

- The inline SVG arrow icon is now consistent across CTAs and avoids text-glyph rendering differences.
- Fixed header offset is now reflected in scroll positioning.
- The implementation has no visible form validation state because contact is handled through mailto/tel links; that is appropriate for the current scope.

## Questions to Consider

- What if the phone hero used the selector as the living object, with the readout changing and the cube reserved for desktop?
- What is the smallest verified operational proof that could sit between the hero and the service fronts?
- Can the desktop pin end just after the three-front state is understood, rather than making visitors finish the full tunnel?
